# Citation & Directory Audit — Drifthook Fly Fishing

**Prepared:** 2 October 2026
**Data sources:** Semrush Backlink Analytics, Semrush Organic Research (US database), public SERP verification
**Decision requested:** proceed with directory outreach, or divert budget to editorial link placements

---

## Recommendation (read this first)

**Do not fund directory outreach. Divert the budget to editorial link placements — and spend the first
portion of it on cleaning up an active link-spam problem that is currently costing more than any
citation gap ever could.**

Three findings drive this, in order of importance:

1. **The link profile is actively deteriorating right now.** Backlinks grew 4.2x since January 2026
   while referring domains *fell* 35% and Authority Score dropped from 29 to 23. That is the signature
   of a spam injection, not growth.
2. **Drifthook is not a local business in the search sense.** It is a national Shopify e-commerce brand.
   It ranks for zero commercial local-intent keywords and appears in zero address packs.
3. **There are no major-directory listings to lose or reclaim.** Prior citation spend did not produce
   listings on the directories that matter — it produced low-quality link-network placements.

**Backlinks up 322% since January. Referring domains down 35% from peak. Authority Score down 8 points.**

Healthy link acquisition raises both counts together. Here, link *volume* is being manufactured from a
shrinking pool of ever-worse domains. The referring-domain list confirms the source: link shorteners
(`atomizelink.icu`, `byteshort.xyz`, `blinks.sbs`, `shortenurls.eu`, `buzzshrink.website`), paid-link
networks (`backlinksbank.com`, `firstguestpost.com`, `best-seo-domains.com`, `highseoonline.com`,
`seo-revealed.club`, `all-aged-domains.com`), and auto-generated content farms (`metamagic.top`,
`blogsphere.top`, `optimizeflow.top`, `creativeposts.top`).

Something began injecting these around **January–February 2026** and is still running — new links are
still appearing with first-seen dates inside the last few weeks.

**This needs to be identified and stopped before any new link budget is committed.** Spending on
editorial placements while this continues is pouring clean water into a leaking bucket.

## 5. Why directory outreach is the wrong spend

Semrush organic data for `drifthook.com` (US):

- **4,792 organic keywords; 848 monthly organic visits; 0 address-pack appearances**
- Top traffic terms are all branded product or national commercial terms —
  *"drifthook fly fishing forceps & bottle opener nipper kit"* (pos. 1), *"fly fishing lure kit set"* (pos. 4),
  *"fly fishing flies kit"* (pos. 7)
- **Every Colorado keyword ranks between position 38 and 78 and delivers zero traffic** —
  *"colorado fly fishing"* (76), *"fly fishing colorado"* (75), *"fly fishing colorado springs"* (60)
- Critically, those Colorado terms all point at **one blog post** —
  `/blogs/discover/top-26-places-to-fly-fish-in-colorado-and-what-flies-to-use`. They are research-intent
  content keywords about *where to fish in Colorado*, not *where to buy from a shop in Westminster*.

Drifthook sells kits nationally by post. Nobody drives to 10316 Owens Street. Citations exist to win
local-pack and proximity rankings — a surface this business does not compete on and has no commercial
reason to. Directory outreach would buy a ranking factor for a query class that generates no revenue.

**One genuine opportunity does sit in this data**, and it is editorial, not directory: the Colorado and
state-by-state guide content already ranks on pages 4–8 for terms with real volume (880, 720, 390/mo).
That content is one link-equity push away from page one. Editorial placements pointed at those guides
would move keywords that already exist, rather than creating listings nobody searches.

## Method and limitations

- Coverage was derived from referring-domain forensics plus direct SERP verification, because Semrush
  has no native citation/NAP scanner. This reliably detects directories that **link** — which the major
  US citation sites do.
- A small number of listings could exist without a crawlable link (rare for the major platforms, and
  none were found in SERP checks). A definitive 70-plus-directory sweep with per-field NAP comparison
  requires a dedicated citation tool such as BrightLocal, Whitespark or Yext. Given that the
  recommendation is not to pursue directories at all, commissioning one is not advised.
- Direct fetches of `drifthook.com` and `tu.org` were blocked by this environment's network egress
  policy; those details were verified through indexed search results instead, and corroborated across
  multiple independent sources.
