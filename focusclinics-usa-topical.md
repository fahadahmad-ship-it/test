authority for the blog; they will not bring patients.

## Three warnings

**`radiologykey.com`, `musculoskeletalkey.com` and `plasticsurgerykey.com` are one network.**
Same `*key.com` medical-content footprint (with `aneskey.com` and `clinicalgate.com`, which
appear on another client's sheet in this account). Buy **at most one** — three purchases here
would read as three links from one source, not three independent domains.

**`clinichunter.com`** is a medical-tourism platform — it exists to route patients to overseas
clinics, including eye surgery. Topically close, but you would be paying to appear on a site
whose business model competes with a London clinic. Worth a look before buying.

**`walkindermatology.com`** is an actual dermatology clinic site. A link from one clinic's site
to another clinic is an unusual editorial pattern and reads as a paid placement. Verify what
the placement actually looks like.

That leaves $335. Adding `rumbie.co` $157 (92,606 traffic, AS 49, TF 38, beauty/skincare) makes

**Or spend it on one:** `flaunt.com` at **$1,017** buys 1,013,056 monthly visits at AS 59 with
an 11 RD/1k ratio — roughly seven times the traffic of the best sub-$300 option, on a single
domain. Beauty and skincare is a defensible adjacency for cosmetic vision correction. If you
want one strong US link rather than three modest ones, this is it.

## How this compares to my earlier three

My previous picks (`medindia.net` $250, `topendsports.com` $340, `islandecho.co.uk` $325) were
built for UK commercial relevance plus health authority. If the goal has shifted to US topical
reach, `medindia.net` (Indian) and `topendsports.com` (Australian-run) are the weaker fits and
the table above serves better. `islandecho.co.uk` still earns its place as the UK signal —
the clinic is in London, and an entirely US profile leaves the paying market unserved.

Metrics are the marketplace sheet's own figures, not re-verified per domain; I contacted no
publisher.
