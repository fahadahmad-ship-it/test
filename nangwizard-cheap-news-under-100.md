# Cheap news sites under $100 — honest assessment

**Short version: this tier is mostly unusable.** Of 64 news domains under $100 that passed
basic quality gates, I can recommend one with confidence and two with reservations.

## How I filtered

Applied a link-farm detector on top of the usual gates: **referring domains per 1,000 monthly
visits**. A genuine local paper earns links roughly in proportion to readership. A site selling
links accumulates hundreds or thousands of referring domains while traffic stays flat.
Above ~400 RD/1k traffic is a strong selling signature.

not calling them farms.

## Verified findings

**`newsatrack.co.uk` ($51) — do not buy.** It looked like the best value in the set on metrics
(AS 41, 111k traffic, clean RD ratio). It is flagged as a high-risk scam domain, is resold
across at least four link vendors at $12–$74, and openly advertises that it accepts crypto and
trading content. Price dispersion that wide across vendors is itself a tell.

**`addmagazine.co.uk` ($30) — skip.** Sold through the same guest-post vendor networks at a
near-identical price point. Generic multi-topic inventory.

**`otsnews.co.uk` ($60) — the one genuine option.** OTS News is a real Southport, Merseyside
local publication: named author pages, a submit-a-story process, a letters-to-the-editor page,
an active Facebook presence. It does sell placements (listed elsewhere at $57–$130), but it is
an actual newspaper rather than a link shell. AS 37, TF 29, 19k traffic.

`sixmagazine.co.uk` ($61) shows 39k traffic but only 358 referring domains — too thin an
authority base to pass a link through. `genius.com` ($50, AS 95) is a user-generated annotation
platform, not a news placement; same trap as the Group D entries.

## No Australian news under $100

There is nothing usable. `anyauto.com.au` ($90) is AS 22 with a suspect ratio,
`philtimes.com.au` ($77) reads as a farm, `smbtech.au` ($93) is tech rather than news.
The cheapest credible AU masthead inventory starts around $110 (`mediaman.com.au`) and
those sit in the high-refusal-risk group anyway.

## The strategic point

News is the wrong angle for this client regardless of price. The competitor link graph shows
Australian mastheads covering nangs as a **public-health harm story**, and NSW restrictions land
5 November 2026. Buying cheap news placements aims a weak link type at the one topic where
editorial resistance is highest. $60 on `otsnews.co.uk` is fine as a filler. The same $60–$300
spent in the Group A food and drink tier — `wine.co.za` $145, `restovisio.com` $150,
`totalbristol.com` $100 — buys real topical relevance instead.
