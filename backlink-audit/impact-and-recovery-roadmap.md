# Business Impact & Link Recovery Roadmap — ngwindows.com

**Companion to:** `ngwindows-backlink-audit.md`
**Date:** 6 October 2026
**Scope:** Quantified business impact of the Jun–Oct 2026 spam/PBN link blast, competitive link
benchmark, and a 90-day link rebuild roadmap.
**Data sources:** Semrush (Domain Overview rank history, Organic Research, Organic Competitors,
Backlink Analytics bulk comparison, referring domains, Authority Score profile); Ahrefs free public
Domain Rating endpoint.

> via the free public endpoint.

## 1. Headline verdict — read this first

**The spam blast has NOT yet caused a measurable loss of rankings or traffic.**

**But ngwindows.com is in a severe organic decline that began roughly twelve months BEFORE the
spam started, and that decline has nothing to do with links.**

These are two separate problems and conflating them would misdirect the entire budget.

| | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Is there a decline?** | Yes — and it is severe. Organic traffic fell **40,569 → 4,971/mo**, a **−87.7%** collapse from the Aug 2024 peak. |
| **Did the spam cause it?** | **No.** The collapse was already ~80% complete before the first spam link landed (29 Jun 2026). |
| **Has the spam hurt yet?** | **No measurable incremental damage.** Traffic bottomed in **Jun 2026 at 3,420** — the month the blast began — and has risen **+45%** since, to 4,971. |
| **Is the spam still a risk?** | **Yes.** The Sep/Oct escalation (+642 referring domains in 8 weeks) is too recent to have been scored. This is **preventive remediation on a real risk**, not emergency triage on proven damage. |
| **Is the link profile the reason they don't rank locally?** | **No.** ngwindows has **more quality referring domains (65 at AS≥30) than either local competitor** (34 and 51). The bottleneck is commercial content, not links. |

**Commercial framing for the client:** do not sell this as "spam links destroyed your traffic."
That is not what the data says, and it would be found out. Sell it as: *"Your traffic has fallen 88%
for reasons that pre-date the spam. Separately, you are now under a link attack that hasn't scored
yet but is still live. We fix the attack cheaply and defensively, and we put the real money into the
commercial-page and local-ranking problem that actually caused the loss."*

### 2.1 Three distinct phases

**Phase 1 — The real collapse (Jun 2025 → May 2026). No spam involved.**
Traffic fell **24,194 → 4,360 = −82.0%** over eleven months. The single sharpest break is
**Jun→Jul 2025: 24,194 → 15,372 (−36.5% in one month)**. The first spam link in the audit's
Network A/B clusters is dated **29 June 2026** — a full **twelve months later**. There is no
causal path from the spam to this decline.

**Phase 2 — The blast window (Jun 2026 → Sep 2026). No incremental damage.**
- Traffic: **3,420 → 4,971 = +45.4%.** Jun 2026 is the lowest month in the entire 30-month series,
  and it is the month the blast *started*. Every month since has been higher.
- Organic keywords: **4,249 → 4,180 = −1.6%.** Flat.
- Semrush Rank: **433,578 → 331,548** — an improvement of 23.5% (lower is better).
- Organic traffic cost (paid-media equivalent): **$35,385 → $54,170/mo = +53%.**

**Phase 3 — The escalation (Sep/Oct 2026). Unmeasured.**
The +266 (Sep) and +376 (Oct) referring-domain surges are the dangerous part, and **they are too
recent to appear in any ranking data.** Sep 2026 traffic (4,971) vs Aug (5,139) is **−3.3%** —
inside normal month-to-month noise for this site, which has swung ±30% repeatedly. October data
does not exist yet. **Any claim that the escalation has or has not hurt is currently unsupportable.
That is precisely why monitoring (§6) matters more than panic.**

### 2.2 The one genuine negative signal

Top-of-SERP presence has continued to erode through the blast window, mildly:

| Position band | Jun 2026 | Sep 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positions 1–3 | 88 | 78 | **−11.4%** |
| Positions 4–10 | 245 | 219 | **−10.6%** |
| Positions 11–20 | 582 | 514 | **−11.7%** |

A uniform ~11% erosion across all three bands, while total keywords held flat (−1.6%) and traffic
rose, reads as **continued gentle slide, not a link penalty**. A link-spam demotion produces a sharp,
asymmetric collapse in the top bands. This is not that shape. But the twelve-month figure is the one
to worry about: **positions 1–3 fell from 226 (Sep 2025) to 78 (Sep 2026), −65.5%.**

### 2.3 What actually caused the decline — hypothesis, flagged as such

The data points away from links and toward **informational content losing its SERP**:

- Traffic is overwhelmingly **blog/informational**. `/blog/standard-door-sizes` alone holds
  **907 ranking keywords** but earns only **225 visits** — a classic "ranks broadly, earns nothing"
  profile, and exactly the content type AI Overviews absorb.
- The informational blog cluster (`standard-door-sizes`, `modern-farmhouse-windows`,
  `palmetto-bugs`, `bow-window-versus-bay-window`, `double-vs-triple-pane`, `cost-to-replace-windows`)
  totals ~**1,062 visits = 22%** of all organic traffic and is made of exactly the queries
  ("how tall is an average door", "water bugs in georgia", "farmhouse windows") that no longer
  generate clicks.
- The timing (Jun–Jul 2025 break) aligns with AI Overview rollout across informational
  home-improvement queries, not with any link event.

**This is a hypothesis, not a finding.** Confirm it in **Google Search Console**: compare
impressions vs clicks for the blog directory across 2025–2026. If impressions held and clicks fell,
it is SERP-feature cannibalisation and no amount of link building will fix it.

### 3.2 The structural problem, in one observation

**`/windows` — the core commercial page of a window replacement company — does not appear in the
top 20 pages at all.** It ranks #11 for "infinity windows" (1,600/mo) and earns **0** traffic from it.

Meanwhile:
- **Homepage + GBP variant = 60.6% of all organic traffic**, and it is almost entirely **branded**.
- The branded keyword set (`ng windows`, `ng window`, `north georgia replacement windows`,
  `north ga windows`, `ngwindows`, plus the `ngwindows.com leadership team` cluster) accounts for
  roughly **2,594 of ~4,820 visits = ~54%** — and that is before counting the branded share
  inside the homepage total.
- **All four service-area pages combined earn ~111 visits/month (2.3%).**

**ngwindows.com is not ranking for its market. It is ranking for its own name.**

**Two conclusions, both uncomfortable for the original audit framing:**

1. **The 82.5% junk ratio is not anomalous — it is the niche norm.** Window World Atlanta sits at
   74.9% and Davis Windows at 80.4%, both with the same AS-2 mega-cluster (431 and 553 domains
   respectively). Auto-generated scraper farms index every small contractor site. The original
   audit called this "the single most damning chart"; on a competitive basis it is a **4–8
   percentage-point** deviation from peers, not an outlier. The spam blast is real and the anchor
   text is damning — but **this particular metric is not the evidence for it.**

2. **ngwindows already has the strongest quality link profile in its local peer group.**
   65 domains at AS≥30, versus 34 for the rival that matches its traffic and 51 for Davis.
   On genuine (AS≥6) referring domains the ranking is **ngwindows 216 · daviswin 157 ·
   windowworldatlanta 176**.

**→ ngwindows is not losing locally because of a link deficit. It leads on links and still only
matches Window World Atlanta on traffic (4,971 vs 4,758) while holding nearly twice the referring
domains.** The gap is commercial content, service-area page depth, and local-pack presence.

### 4.4 What this means for budget

| Spend | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Disavow + monitoring | **Yes — do it, it is cheap and the risk is real.** A few hours of work. |
| Large-scale link acquisition to "catch up" | **No — there is nothing to catch up to locally.** |
| Targeted quality links (20–30 relevant, local, AS 30+) | **Yes** — to push the page-two commercial cluster (§3.3) over the line, and to dilute the spam ratio. |
| Commercial page + local SEO rebuild | **This is where the money should go.** Out of scope for this document — flag it to the client. |

### 4.5 The realistic target number

To move from 5.3% quality domains to a defensible ~10% while diluting the spam, and to close the
gap with the national players' *relevance* (not their volume):

**Target: +20 to +30 referring domains at AS≥30, local or industry-relevant, over 90 days.**
That would take ngwindows from 65 → 85–95 quality domains, roughly **2.5× Window World Atlanta's
entire quality profile.** This is achievable through the named opportunities below without
buying a single link.

### 5.5 Anchor-text policy for all new links

With ~4,894 spam links carrying commercial anchors already in the profile, the rebuild must run
**defensively branded**:

| Anchor type | Target share |
|---|---|
| Branded (`North Georgia Replacement Windows`, `NG Windows`) | **60%** |
| Naked URL (`ngwindows.com`) | 25% |
| Generic (`visit website`, `learn more`) | 15% |
| **Exact-match commercial** (`window replacement atlanta`) | **0% until the disavow has settled (min. 6 months)** |

Most of the §5.2–5.4 targets are directories and press, which produce branded anchors by default.
This policy is mostly automatic — but brief any PR agency explicitly.

### 5.6 What NOT to do

- **Do not buy "high DA" links, guest posts, or niche edits.** Whatever the origin of the current
  blast, a second unnatural pattern on top of an active disavow is how a manual action happens.
- **Do not chase link volume.** The benchmark (§4.3) shows volume is already a non-issue locally.
- **Do not disavow the Singapore scraper farms first.** They are Network C, low risk. Prioritise
  Networks A and B (the dofollow PBN and fake-guest-post clusters).
- **Do not promise the client that disavowing will recover the traffic.** It will not. The traffic
  was lost before the spam existed.

## 7. Summary for the client

1. **Your organic traffic is down 88% from its August 2024 peak** (40,569 → 4,971 visits/month).
   That is the real problem and it needs addressing.
2. **The spam links did not cause it.** The decline ran from mid-2025 through mid-2026. The first
   spam link landed **29 June 2026** — by which point roughly 80% of the loss had already happened.
   Traffic has actually **risen 45%** since the blast began.
3. **The spam is still a genuine risk, and it is still arriving** (+376 referring domains in
   October alone). The September/October escalation is too recent to have been scored by Google.
   **We remediate it now because it is cheap and the downside is severe — not because it has
   already hurt you.**
4. **Links are not why you lose to local competitors.** You hold **65 quality referring domains
   (AS 30+) versus 34 for Window World Atlanta and 51 for Davis Windows.** You lead your local
   market on link quality and still only match Window World Atlanta on traffic.
5. **The actual gap is commercial.** 60% of your organic traffic comes from people searching your
   own company name. Your `/windows` page earns effectively nothing.
   **`window replacement atlanta` ($41 per click) sits at position 15.** Four service-area pages
   together earn 111 visits a month.
6. **The 90-day plan is therefore:** disavow the spam cheaply and defensively; acquire **20–30
   genuinely local, genuinely relevant links** that you currently lack and your competitors have
   (starting with the **fact that you hold no Georgia chamber of commerce link at all** — your only
   chamber link is from **Tennessee**); and push the real budget toward the commercial-page and
   local-ranking work that will actually recover revenue.
