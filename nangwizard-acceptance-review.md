# nangwizard.net — will these 66 sites actually accept the link?

## How I tested this, and what I could not test

**Hard evidence I gathered**

1. Pulled the referring domains of nangwizard's own organic competitors — `nangsdelivery.com`,
   `nangsboy.com.au`, `mrnang.com.au`, `nosboss.net`, `supremewhip.com.au` and others — to find
   publishers with a *proven* record of carrying a nangs link.
2. **Result: zero of the 66 has ever linked to a nangs site.** There is no proven acceptance
   anywhere in this list. Every verdict below is a graded likelihood, not a confirmation.
3. What the competitor link graph *does* contain is telling: `smh.com.au`, `theage.com.au`,
   `brisbanetimes.com.au`, `watoday.com.au`, `sbs.com.au`, `abc.net.au`, `thenewdaily.com.au`.
   Those are **news stories about nangs as a public-health problem**, not paid placements.
   Australian mastheads cover this niche as harm reporting.

**The regulatory fact that changes the maths**

NSW announced a nitrous oxide crackdown in July 2026; the restrictions take effect
**5 November 2026** — about five weeks away. Tanks over 10g become illegal to sell, bulbs are
capped at 250g per customer per day, and under-18 supply is banned outright, with a carve-out
for food and beverage businesses. Sydney is nangwizard's second-biggest city page.
Australian editors are actively writing about this right now, which is exactly why the AU
mastheads in Group C are the *least* likely to take a commercial nangs link despite being
the best topical and geographic fit on paper.

**What I could not verify**

I did not contact any publisher. I could not read the broker's restricted-niche list — the
supplied sheet has no such column, so whether Reachgorilla itself blocks nitrous oxide
inventory is unknown and worth asking them directly before you plan spend.

## The framing that decides most of these

Culinary use of cream chargers stays legal in every Australian state. Acceptance rates will
differ enormously depending on what you pitch:

- **Works:** "cream chargers", "whipped cream chargers", "N2O for home baking", linking to a
  guide page such as `/guides/how-to-fix-runny-whipped-cream/`.
- **Gets rejected:** "nangs delivery Sydney", "nang delivery", anything linking the money page
  with a delivery-intent anchor.

Pitch the culinary guides, not the delivery pages. That single choice will move acceptance
more than any site on this list.

**Buy first:** `cheese.com` $400, `therooftopguide.com` $360, `angsarap.net` $237,
`restovisio.com` $150, `wine.co.za` $145 — all Group A, all real editorial food sites.

**Re-check before buying:** the Group D "bargains" (`houzz.com.au` $36, `gardenweb.com` $70,
`evite.com` $260) look like the best value in the whole dataset purely on AS/TF/traffic. They
are cheap because the link is a user profile, forum post or syndicated feed item, not an
editorial placement. The metrics are real; the link value is not.

**Ask the broker one question before committing:** does Reachgorilla accept nitrous oxide /
cream charger content, and on which of these specific domains? A single email removes most of
the uncertainty in this document.
