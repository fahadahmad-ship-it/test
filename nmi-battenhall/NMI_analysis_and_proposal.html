NMI reaches 46 percent of Authorize.Net's US traffic and 82 percent of its keyword footprint from one eighth of the referring domains.

And the direction of travel favours you. Over the last twelve months NMI added 113 referring domains a month. Authorize.Net lost 315 a month, down 3,780 on the year, and has not recovered its October peak.

Two things worth saying plainly

The Authority Score gap is wider than it looks. Three points between 46 and 49 is not three units of work. The scale is logarithmic, so it represents thousands of referring domains and each further point costs more than the last. The score on its own makes the distance look far shorter than it is.

Why this plan counts websites and not links. NMI's profile shows 22.8 million backlinks, which sounds enormous. They come from fewer than 5,000 websites. The NMI badge sits on merchant checkout pages, so it is counted again on every page it appears on. To a search engine, one website linking a thousand times is still one website. Adyen averages 24 links per website. NMI averages 4,605.

02  What we found

Two things nobody is watching.

One is a live spam network pointed at the homepage. The other is a list of publications already linking to brands NMI owns, and never to NMI.

One. An active spam network, still growing

Arriving at roughly 170 domains a month, with 72 percent of it landed in 2026 alone. Around 95 percent points at the homepage. Three clusters, none of them subtle: domains that openly sell links, running between Domain Rating 53 and 73 with no traffic at all; a single network of more than 100 domains on one commercial top level domain, every one at Domain Rating 32 with no traffic; and a directory network between 46 and 54.

The anchor text has moved through five generations since October 2025, each keyed to a different NMI brand including the absorbed ones, so whoever built it scraped the full portfolio.

A filter set on authority alone passes all 327 of these domains. Adding a traffic threshold removes almost all of them.

This is why we screen on traffic and relevance first and treat authority score as the last check rather than the first.

Two. Publications that link to brands you own, and never to you

NMI has absorbed USAePay, IRIS CRM, Creditcall, CRM Dialer and Agreement Express. Four of the five still run as live properties with their own rankings, which is a reasonable commercial decision and not one this plan questions.

Creditcall alone earned coverage in American Banker, TechRadar, BetaNews and The Next Web. None of it points at NMI.

Those are the same publications this plan targets. The difference is that NMI already has a relationship with each of them through a brand it owns, which turns a cold pitch into a warm one.

The five domains hold 2,081, 962, 780, 693 and 686 referring domains. Those totals overstate the opportunity and we would rather say so. They overlap, around half already link to NMI as well, and much of the remainder is merchant checkout integration and link shorteners rather than editorial coverage. One pet adoption site alone accounts for 205 links to USAePay.

Filtered to properties with real traffic that do not already link to NMI, the usable list is roughly 180 domains. We are not proposing to redirect or retire those brand properties. That is a migration decision with merchant and support implications and it sits outside an offsite programme.

For balance, your good links are genuinely good

783 referring domains carry a thousand or more monthly organic visits, including Wikipedia, Adobe, GitHub, Forbes, Shopify, Salesforce, Bloomberg, Mastercard and PYMNTS. Roughly one in five. There are simply not many of them and almost all point at the homepage rather than at anything that sells.

We have not assessed penalty risk and are not claiming any. Establishing whether the spam network is doing measurable harm is audit work rather than snapshot work.

The flagship names are the reason this is a curated plan. A month with Business Insider, Investing.com or Reuters carries fewer placements, because one genuine national title is worth more to a payments brand than several mid tier ones. In a lighter month the count rises and the flagship rests. The quality floor never moves.

Names we shortlisted and pulled, because NMI already has them: Inc, ZDNet, G2, Crunchbase, Forbes, TechCrunch, Fortune, Entrepreneur and PYMNTS.

Those move to a retention list. Buying again a link NMI already earned is the easiest money an agency can waste, and we would not.

The names that cannot be bought, and how we get them anyway

The genuine payments trade press, American Banker, Payments Dive, Finextra and Tearsheet, does not sell placements. Those are earned through digital PR built on NMI's own data, which is why the research asset sits in the plan. And several publications already link to Creditcall, a brand NMI owns, which turns a cold pitch into a warm introduction.

How each tier is actually secured

  • Flagship and editorial names come through established publisher relationships. That is why we can name and hold them rather than list what we hope to reach.
  • Developer registries and directories are largely free, and several need a package, SDK listing or built integration from NMI first. We tell you which, you decide if it is worth the engineering time.
  • Payments peer placements sit with NMI's business development team, not with our outreach. We identify and brief them, you open the door.

How every domain is vetted

Eleven checks per domain, in this order: organic traffic and where it comes from, traffic trend, topical relevance, link type, Authority Score, the site's own referring domain profile, outbound link pattern, anchor distribution, indexation of the page the link will sit on, domain history, and spam signals. Traffic comes first rather than last, because it is the check that catches networks passing on score.

We do not place on private blog networks, on domains with no organic traffic whatever their score reads, on thin directories, or in sitewide and footer positions. NMI already has 22.8 million of the last kind.

04  Delivery

Reclaim first, then build.

Three workstreams

One. Reclaim before acquiring. Warm outreach to the roughly 180 publications that link to a brand NMI owns and have never linked to NMI. Lost link reclamation on a quarterly pass. Spam disavow in banded batches, measured before and after. Two further items sit on the site and outside this plan, so we flag rather than propose them: the duplicate privacy pages holding around 1,900 referring domains across five versions, and any decision about the absorbed brand domains.

Two. Build against proven targets. The 78 actionable domains, prioritised by route. Developer registries and integration directories first, because they match who NMI sells to and cost the least to earn.

Three. Earn with the formats that work here. A payment flow diagram, the cheapest asset in the category, and one a competitor equivalent holds 610 referring domains from. A PayFac and ISO glossary hub, since explainers are the most linked format across all three competitors. Original research each quarter, which NMI's own history supports: the generative AI piece earned 143 referring domains without being built for it.

What to expect

Built from the measured link gap on named pages rather than a click through assumption. Closing a link gap is within our control. Where a page lands afterwards also depends on what competitors do in the same window, so these are expectations rather than guarantees.

We will not forecast a site wide traffic percentage. Login and brand queries account for a large share of NMI's traffic, and AI Overviews appear on seven of the eight priority terms, so any headline number would be chosen to look good rather than defended. Page level movement on named terms is measurable and is what we would be held to.

Measurement

What we report

  • Editorial referring domains won each month, by authority and traffic band
  • Referring domains gained per target page
  • Position movement on the named terms
  • Spam domain count, tracked down rather than ignored

What we would not report

  • Total backlinks. 22.8 million of them are one integration counted repeatedly
  • Total referring domains. The profile gains around 113 a month unaided and a growing share is spam
  • Total organic traffic. Login and brand queries account for much of it

Before month one we set a baseline separating editorially earned domains from the badge footprint and the spam, so the programme is not measured against movement it did not create.

Why this starts now

The spam network adds roughly 500 domains a quarter, so every quarter of delay is more to assess against a baseline that gets harder to read. Authorize.Net is down 3,780 referring domains on the year and has not recovered its peak, and a gap closes fastest while a competitor is weak. Link equity decays continuously, so the warm list is worth more now than in twelve months.

What we would need to start

  • Who owns on site changes, since the product page sequence depends on the overlap being resolved
  • Whether we can reference the absorbed brands in outreach, since several publications know NMI through Creditcall or USAePay rather than by name
  • Your product pages in commercial priority order, to check against ours
  • Any link history worth knowing, including prior agencies, disavow files and manual actions

This is a snapshot rather than an audit.
