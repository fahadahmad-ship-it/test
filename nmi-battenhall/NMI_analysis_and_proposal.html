NMI is the only one of the four gaining share against a shrinking competitor. That changes the objective from catching up to taking ground.

Being straight about the Authority Score gap

NMI scores 46 against 48 for Authorize.Net and 49 for Adyen. That looks like a short distance and it is not.

Authority Score is logarithmic. Moving from 46 to 49 is not three units of work, it represents thousands of referring domains, and each further point costs more than the last. Anyone presenting that gap as nearly closed is reading the scale wrong.

The honest measure is the link count. NMI holds roughly one twelfth of Adyen's referring domains and one eighth of Authorize.Net's. Closing that is a multi year build.

What makes the position winnable anyway

Two things, and neither is the score. Authorize.Net is going backwards. And on the terms NMI can realistically win, results are decided at page level where the bar is very low. Section 02 measures that bar per keyword rather than generalising it.

The number that misleads

NMI shows 22.8 million backlinks, second only to Stripe here and sixteen times Adyen. That describes one thing rather than many. It is the badge sitting on merchant checkouts, repeated across millions of pages, and it comes from under 5,000 domains.

How to read the figures. Authority Score is Semrush. Referring domains, backlinks, traffic, keywords and anchors are Ahrefs, live links, subdomains mode. The two tools index differently and disagree on totals, so Semrush reports 4,968 referring domains for NMI where Ahrefs reports roughly 4,100. Every table above states its source, and no figure mixes the two. Traffic value estimates what the same visits would cost through paid search. It is not revenue.

The uncomfortable part of the brief

The keyword direction we were given names payment processing and payment processor. NMI does not rank in the top 100 for either, nor for payment gateway or merchant services. There is no page to push.

Those results are held by legacy incumbents at a median Domain Rating between 87 and 95, with ranking pages carrying 44 to 58 referring domains each. Winning there needs a page built and established first. Links sent at those terms today buy nothing, and we would rather say that now than report it in month six.

We would still serve the intent behind the brief. The commercial demand those terms represent is reachable through the mid tail, where NMI already ranks and where the bar is a tenth as high.

What AI Overviews change

AI Overviews now appear on seven of these eight priority terms and on roughly 34 of the 40 terms in the wider cluster. Payment gateway is the only high volume head term without one.

Assume meaningful click suppression on informational queries. Click per search sits below 1.0 on merchant services and payment gateway, so headline volume overstates the traffic available. Any forecast built on volume alone will disappoint, and ours will not be.

Product pages. The constraint is not links

The brief asks for links to product pages. Before spending anything there, two findings change what that money should do.

Adyen's flagship online payments page carries 47 referring domains. NMI's payments product page carries 57 and its gateway page carries 55.

NMI's product pages already out link Adyen's. Volume is not what is holding them back.

The clearest proof sits inside NMI's own site. The ScanX underwriting page holds 227 referring domains and produces no organic traffic at all. Links are not the missing input.

What is actually happening. NMI outranks itself

The blog owns the commercial clusters the product pages should own.

  • White label. The blog guide holds thirteen white label terms including the money variants at positions 2, 3 and 5. The gateway product page is left with a single 200 volume term. Three NMI URLs share that result, four counting a whitepaper.
  • Text to pay. The blog guide holds the head term at position 6 and shares the secondary term with the product page. Three NMI URLs.
  • Unattended payments. Six NMI URLs on one result, and a blog post ranks above the solutions page.
  • Merchant onboarding. The blog holds the head term at position 5 and ties the product page on the software variant.
  • Fraud. The blog holds payment fraud prevention at position 1, ahead of the fraud product page.

Sending links at those product pages before resolving the overlap pushes against the site's own internal competition. The sequence matters more than the volume.

It has run through five generations of anchor text since October 2025, each one a different link selling brand. Notably it targets NMI's acquired brand names as often as NMI's own, which means whoever built it scraped the full brand portfolio.

A filter set on authority alone passes all 327. Adding a traffic threshold removes almost all of them. That is why we screen on traffic and relevance first and treat score as a guide.

06  Proposal

Three workstreams. The first needs no outreach.

One. Reclaim what is already yours

Before acquiring a single new link, there is recoverable equity sitting unused.

  • Redirect the five acquired brand properties. 4,962 referring domains currently point at usaepay.com, iriscrm.com, creditcall.com, crmdialer.com and agreementexpress.com rather than NMI. Mapped redirects consolidate that equity and bring in 181 net new quality referring domains with no prospecting.
  • Consolidate the duplicate privacy pages. Roughly 1,900 referring domains are split across five versions of the same policy page.
  • Reclaim lost links. 68 percent of all referring domains ever recorded are now dead. A quarterly pass recovers the recoverable ones.
  • Begin disavowing the spam network in banded batches, starting with recent followed placements, measured before and after.

This workstream is the highest return in the programme and the least glamorous. It runs first and continues in the background.

Two. Build against proven targets

Not prospecting from scratch. The 29 confirmed gaps are places a direct competitor already holds a link, so relevance and willingness are both evidenced before we make contact. The wider pool behind them runs into the low hundreds.

Priority order follows the audience rather than the metric. Developer registries and integration directories first, because they match who NMI sells to and because competitors demonstrably hold them. Trade press, comparison sites and standards bodies follow.

Screening

A prospect must clear all four gates.

  • Real traffic. 1,000 or more monthly organic visits. This is the primary gate, because it is the one that catches networks passing on score.
  • Relevance. Payments, fintech, ecommerce, developer or adjacent business technology.
  • Profile health. Clean outbound pattern, real editorial function, no link selling footprint.
  • Authority. Score 30 and above as a guide. It is the last check, not the first, and we would take a relevant site with real readers scoring 20 over an empty one scoring 50.

Three. Earn links with the formats that work here

Section 05 shows what gets cited in payments. We would build against that evidence rather than a generic content plan.

  • A payment flow diagram. The single cheapest asset in the category. A competitor equivalent holds 606 referring domains.
  • A PayFac and ISO glossary hub. Explainer pages are the most linked format across all three competitors, and this is the cluster NMI can win outright.
  • Developer documentation as a link asset. NMI already has the developer pages. They are not currently built or promoted to be cited.
  • Original research, once a quarter. NMI's own history supports this. The generative AI piece earned 143 referring domains and the consumer survey earned 42, without either being built for that purpose.

Measurement

What we commit to

  • Editorial referring domains won each month, by authority band and by traffic band
  • Referring domains gained per target page against section 03
  • Position movement on the named target keywords
  • Spam domain count, tracked down rather than ignored

What we would advise against

  • Total backlinks. 22.8 million of them are one integration counted repeatedly
  • Total referring domains. The profile already gains around 112 a month unaided, and a growing share of that is spam
  • Total organic traffic. Login and brand queries account for a large share of it

Before month one we would establish a baseline separating editorially earned domains from the badge footprint and the spam network. Without that split the programme gets measured against movement it did not create, in either direction.

What we would need to start

  • Confirmation of who owns on site changes, since the product page sequence depends on overlap being resolved first
  • Ownership status of the five acquired brand domains and whether redirects are possible
  • Product pages ranked in commercial priority order, to sanity check ours against yours
  • Any link history worth knowing about, including prior agencies, existing disavow files and manual actions
  • Confirmation of target markets beyond the United States

This is a snapshot rather than an audit.
