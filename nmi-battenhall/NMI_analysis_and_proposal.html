A quarter of your link profile is spam, and it is still growing.

Roughly 1,170 of NMI's referring domains carry a spam flag, with around 170 more arriving every month. A further 5,202 point at brands NMI has already absorbed rather than at NMI itself. Neither is being managed, and both are fixable without a single new placement.

NMI reaches 46 percent of Authorize.Net's US traffic and 82 percent of its keyword footprint from one eighth of the referring domains.

And the direction of travel favours you

Over the last twelve months NMI added 113 referring domains a month, up 49.7 percent. Adyen added 244, up 4.7 percent. Authorize.Net lost 315 a month and is down 12.1 percent, having fallen from a peak of 31,842 last October to 27,361 without recovering.

Two things worth saying plainly

The Authority Score gap is wider than it looks. Three points between 46 and 49 is not three units of work. The scale is logarithmic, so it represents thousands of referring domains and each further point costs more than the last.

Why this plan counts websites and not links. NMI's profile shows 22.8 million backlinks, which sounds enormous. They come from fewer than 5,000 websites. The NMI badge sits on merchant checkout pages, so it gets counted again on every page it appears on. To a search engine, one website linking a thousand times is still one website. Adyen averages 24 links per website. NMI averages 4,605. Every figure in this plan counts websites, because that is the number that moves rankings.

It has run through five generations of anchor text since October 2025, each one a different link selling brand, and it is arriving at roughly 170 domains a month. Around 95 percent of it points at the homepage. It targets NMI's absorbed brand names as often as NMI's own, so whoever built it scraped the full portfolio.

A filter set on authority alone passes all 327 of these domains. Adding a traffic threshold removes almost all of them.

This is why we screen on traffic and relevance first and treat authority score as the last check rather than the first.

For balance, the good links are genuinely good

783 referring domains carry a thousand or more monthly organic visits, including Wikipedia, Adobe, GitHub, Forbes, Shopify, Salesforce, Bloomberg, Mastercard and PYMNTS. Roughly one in five. There are simply not many of them and almost all point at the homepage rather than at anything that sells.

We have not assessed penalty risk and are not claiming any. Establishing whether the spam network is doing measurable harm is audit work, not snapshot work.

Tier one is the immediate opportunity. NMI already ranks between second and fourth on all three terms, and the pages beating it carry between four and eight referring domains. That is a handful of links, not a campaign.

Tier three is where the volume sits, and it is held by incumbents whose ranking pages carry 44 to 58 referring domains against a top ten averaging a Domain Rating in the high eighties. Those terms need a page built and established before links have anything to lift.

04  Which pages

Your product pages do not need more links.

They need the blog to stop outranking them. That sequencing decision is worth more than any number of placements.

Adyen's flagship online payments page carries 49 referring domains. NMI's payments page carries 64 and its gateway page carries 66.

NMI's product pages already out link Adyen's. Volume is not the constraint.

The clearest proof is inside NMI's own site. The ScanX underwriting page holds 313 referring domains and earns no organic traffic at all.

What is actually happening

The blog owns the commercial clusters the product pages should own. On white label terms the blog guide holds thirteen variants including the valuable ones, while the gateway product page is left with a single low volume term. Three NMI URLs share that one result. The same pattern repeats on text to pay, unattended payments, merchant onboarding and fraud, where six NMI URLs compete against each other on a single term.

Sending links at those product pages before resolving the overlap pushes against NMI's own internal competition.

06  The link plan

Three tiers, each doing a different job.

Every candidate clears traffic and relevance before authority is looked at. That order is deliberate, and section 02 shows why.

The screening standard

Four gates, applied in this order.

  • Real organic traffic. A thousand monthly visits as the floor. This is the first gate rather than the last, because it is the one that catches networks passing on score. It is also the gate that would have caught all 327 spam domains in section 02.
  • Topical relevance. Payments, fintech, ecommerce, developer tooling or adjacent business technology.
  • Profile health. A real editorial function, sane outbound linking, and no link selling footprint.
  • Authority. Semrush Authority Score 30 as the floor, checked last.

Tier A. Authority lift

What it is. National business and technology press, major finance media, research and data platforms.

Why it is here. Section 01 showed that domain authority is what gates the tier three head terms, and it is the one thing page level links cannot buy. This tier is the only lever on it.

Where it points. The homepage and the glossary hub. Domain level authority rather than page level movement.

Anchor. Branded and naked URL. These are editorial placements and the anchor is not the point of them.

This is the smallest share of the mix. These placements are the slowest to earn and a small number does the job.

Tier B. Topical relevance

What it is. Payments and fintech trade press, ecommerce media, developer publications, industry analysts.

Why it is here. Section 03 showed the winnable terms are decided at page level on four to eight referring domains. This tier is what supplies them, and topical relevance is what those results reward.

Where it points. The four blog pages in section 04 and the tier one keyword pages.

Anchor. Branded and partial match. This is where the topical signal is carried.

This is the largest share and the engine of the programme.

Tier C. Ecosystem and diversity

What it is. Developer registries and documentation, integration and app directories, technology profilers, comparison and review sites, associations and standards bodies.

Why it is here. Section 05 found 29 of these that a direct competitor holds and NMI does not. They match the people NMI sells to, most are earned rather than bought, and they return qualified referral traffic alongside authority.

Where it points. Product and solutions pages, and the developer documentation.

Anchor. Branded and navigational. Directory and listing anchors are usually fixed anyway.

Substantial share, and the fastest part of the plan to build.

07  Delivery

Three workstreams. The first needs no outreach.

One. Reclaim what is already yours

Redirect the five absorbed brand properties, which consolidates 5,202 referring domains and brings in roughly 180 net new quality domains with no prospecting. Consolidate the duplicate privacy pages, where around 1,900 referring domains are split across five versions of the same page. Begin disavowing the spam network in banded batches, measured before and after.

This is the highest return work in the programme and the least glamorous. It runs first.

Two. Build against proven targets

Not prospecting from scratch. The 29 confirmed gaps are places a direct competitor already holds a link, so relevance and willingness are evidenced before we make contact. Developer registries and integration directories come first because they match who NMI sells to.

Every prospect clears four gates. Real organic traffic, which is the primary filter because it is the one that catches networks passing on score. Topical relevance. A clean outbound profile. Then authority score, as the last check rather than the first.

Three. Earn links with the formats that work

A payment flow diagram, the cheapest asset in the category. A PayFac and ISO glossary hub, since explainers are the most linked format across all three competitors and this is the cluster NMI can win outright. Developer documentation built to be cited. Original research each quarter, which NMI's own history supports, since the generative AI piece earned 143 referring domains without being built for that purpose.

What good looks like in twelve months

  • Top three on payfac and white label payment gateway, both currently fourth with the bar within reach
  • First place defended on embedded payments, where NMI already leads Stripe and Adyen
  • Mobile payments, unattended and fraud ranking on their own head terms rather than on variants
  • Roughly 180 quality referring domains reclaimed and the absorbed brand equity consolidated
  • The spam baseline documented and shrinking rather than growing
  • A first set of citable assets live, on the formats that demonstrably earn links here

How we would measure it

What we report

  • Editorial referring domains won each month, by authority and traffic band
  • Referring domains gained per target page
  • Position movement on the named terms
  • Spam domain count, tracked down rather than ignored

What we would not report

  • Total backlinks. 22.8 million of them are one integration counted repeatedly
  • Total referring domains. The profile gains around 113 a month unaided and a growing share is spam
  • Total organic traffic. Login and brand queries account for much of it

Before month one we would set a baseline separating editorially earned domains from the badge footprint and the spam. Without that split the programme gets measured against movement it did not create.

Why this starts now

The spam network adds roughly 500 domains a quarter, so every quarter of delay is more to assess against a baseline that gets harder to read. Authorize.Net is down 3,780 referring domains year on year and has not recovered its October peak, and a gap closes fastest while a competitor is weak. Link equity decays continuously, so the roughly 180 recoverable domains are worth more now than in twelve months.

What we would need to start

  • Who owns on site changes, since the product page sequence depends on the overlap being resolved
  • Ownership of the five absorbed brand domains and whether redirects are possible
  • Your product pages in commercial priority order, to check against ours
  • Any link history worth knowing, including prior agencies, disavow files and manual actions

This is a snapshot rather than an audit.

The full anchor and toxicity review, the spam network domain by domain, a disavow file, the complete prospect pool and the redirect mapping project sit in a separate piece of work.
