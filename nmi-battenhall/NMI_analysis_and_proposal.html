NMI reaches 46 percent of Authorize.Net's US traffic and 82 percent of its keyword footprint from one eighth of the referring domains.

And momentum favours you. Over the last twelve months NMI added 113 referring domains a month. Authorize.Net lost 315 a month and is down 3,780 on the year.

Two things worth saying plainly

The Authority Score gap is wider than it looks. Three points between 46 and 49 is not three units of work. The scale is logarithmic, so it stands for thousands of referring domains and each further point costs more than the last. The score alone makes the distance look far shorter than it is.

The backlink count is misleading. NMI shows 22.8 million backlinks from fewer than 5,000 websites. The NMI badge sits on merchant checkout pages, so it is counted again on every page it appears on. To a search engine, one website linking a thousand times is still one website. Adyen averages 24 links per website. NMI averages 4,605. Every figure in this document counts websites, not links.

02  What we found

A spam network nobody is watching.

It would pass any check built on authority alone. It fails the moment you look at traffic, which is exactly how we screen.

Arriving at roughly 170 domains a month, with 72 percent of it landed in 2026 alone. Around 95 percent points at the homepage. Three clusters, none of them subtle: domains that openly sell links, running between Domain Rating 53 and 73 with no traffic at all; a single network of more than 100 domains on one commercial top level domain, every one at Domain Rating 32 with no traffic; and a directory network between 46 and 54. The anchor text has moved through five generations since October 2025, each a different link selling brand.

A filter set on authority alone passes all 327 of these domains. Adding a traffic threshold removes almost all of them.

Getting them off the profile needs a full backlink audit and a disavow, which is the first thing we would do.

One anchor in five is spam or link selling text, the same network showing up again. And genuine commercial anchors, the payments terms NMI wants to rank for, are barely 2 percent, so there is no optimised footprint to unbalance. That is wide, safe headroom for new links.

For balance, your good links are genuinely good

783 referring domains carry a thousand or more monthly organic visits, including Wikipedia, Adobe, GitHub, Forbes, Shopify, Salesforce, Bloomberg, Mastercard and PYMNTS. Roughly one in five. There are simply not many of them, and almost all point at the homepage rather than at anything that sells.

We have not assessed penalty risk and are not claiming any. Establishing whether the spam is doing measurable harm is part of the full audit.

The larger prize sits behind them

On the head terms, payment processing, payment processor, payment gateway and merchant services, NMI does not yet hold a ranking page at all. Those searches run to 4,500, 3,100, 4,600 and 9,600 a month. Capturing them needs a page built and established first, then links, so they come later in the plan. The route to them runs through the eight pages above, because the topical authority is the same.

The product pages are a different job

The brief names product pages alongside the blog, and they are the second target. But they are not short of links. NMI's gateway and payments product pages already carry 66 and 64 referring domains, more than Adyen's flagship at 49, so piling more links on them moves little. The constraint there is relevance and how the pages compete with each other, not volume. The audit decides which product pages earn a link and which are held, so the budget goes where it changes a ranking.

How we would get there

  1. Full audit and disavowThe complete backlink audit, the spam network domain by domain, and a disavow file to get roughly 1,170 flagged domains off the profile before new links land on a clean base.
  2. Confirm where the links goThe audit shows which pages to push and the term each should own, and resolves where NMI's own pages currently compete with each other. That is what tells us where a link moves a ranking and where it would be wasted.
  3. Build the linksThe Custom and Curated programmes in section 04, pointed at the pages above.

Links are step three, not step one. Sending links at a profile that is a quarter spam, or at pages that compete with each other, wastes the budget. The audit is what makes the link build pay.

What every domain has to clear

Real organic traffic weighted to the United States, a topical fit with payments, fintech or ecommerce, a followed in content link, a clean outbound profile, and Authority Score 30 as an absolute floor. Traffic is the first gate, not the last, because it is the one that catches the spam network in section 02. We do not use private blog networks, thin directories, sitewide or footer placements, or any site without real traffic whatever its score reads.

These two programmes are the shape of the build. The complete audit sets the final plan.
