The network runs at DR 32–74. It would pass almost any score-based screening. Nearly every domain in it has zero organic traffic.

An authority-score filter passes all 327 of these domains. Adding "1,000+ organic traffic" removes 315 of them.

That is why we screen on score and traffic and anchor profile — never score alone. NMI's own data is the proof.

Flag 1 — A third-party spam anchor is live on the profile

An anchor advertising a black-hat link service via Telegram appears across 100 referring domains, first seen October 2025 and still active. NMI did not place these. At current volume the risk is low — but it is unmonitored and it is growing.

Flag 2 — A cluster of misspelled brand-variant anchors needs ownership verification

Around a dozen typo-variants of NMI's acquired brands (USAePay, Network Merchants) appear with suspiciously uniform link volumes. These are either NMI's own defensive registrations or a third-party network. We can determine which — it needs an ownership check.

We have not assessed penalty risk or impact, and we are not claiming any. That assessment is part of a full audit.

Every competitor holds developer links. NMI holds none.

NMI sells embedded payments to US developers. Adyen, Authorize.Net, Checkout.com and Paysafe all carry links from package registries, integration directories, technology profilers and developer communities. NMI carries none of them.

We ran this two ways — a Semrush backlink matrix and an independent Ahrefs referring-domain diff. Both produced the same finding. Two datasets reaching the same answer is the difference between an observation and a finding.

Because they don't need volume. /products/payment-gateway/ and /products/nmi-payments/ already hold 63 and 57 referring domains — above the bar these SERPs set. Quality floor: AS 30 minimum, majority AS 40+, every domain screened for real organic traffic. Below AS 30 a link does not move the metric you report on, and we would not place it.

On the fourth — NMI's own history answers the format question

Their generative-AI-in-payments piece earned 143 referring domains. Their consumer survey earned 42. Original data is what works for this brand, competitors cannot copy it, and it turns the overlap between our workstreams into a reason to run them together rather than in parallel.

What we would deprioritise

  • Badge, footer and template placements. NMI already carries 22.8 million backlinks from 4,968 domains. Adding more of the same moves nothing.
  • Low-authority directory submissions. The bottom band is already 57% of the profile.
  • Anything that adds backlink count without adding a new referring domain at AS 30+.

A quick win we would hand over regardless

Roughly 1,900 of NMI's referring domains point at five duplicate privacy-policy URLs. Consolidating them is free authority that makes every subsequent link work harder. It sits outside this scope — but it should be done, and we will show you exactly where.

How we would measure it

We commit to

  • Editorial referring domains per month, by AS tier
  • Referring domain gain per target page
  • Position movement on named target keywords

We would advise against

  • Total backlinks — 22.8m of them are one integration counted over and over
  • Total referring domains — the profile already gains ~113/month passively
  • Total organic traffic — login pages alone pull 4,000+ visits/month
