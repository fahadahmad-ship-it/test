The Authority Score gap is three points, not thirty.

Measured on Semrush Authority Score, NMI sits within three points of both named competitors. That single fact changes what a link programme can realistically target.

The real distance sits in referring domain count. NMI has roughly 8 times fewer than Adyen and 12 times fewer than Authorize.Net. Stripe is a different order of magnitude and we would not scope against it.

Who actually competes in the results

Stripe, Adyen and Authorize.Net are the brands NMI competes with commercially. They are not always the pages NMI competes with in search. On the target terms the pages ranking above NMI include GoCardless, Adobe, Helcim, SVB, WEX and Wikipedia. We track both sets, because the second one determines rankings and the first one determines how progress gets judged internally.

The profile is proportionally cleaner than both competitors.

17.8 percent of NMI's referring domains sit at Authority Score 30 or above, against 7.9 percent for Adyen and 10.3 percent for Authorize.Net.

This is a scale problem, not a clean up problem, and that is a far better place to start from.

Score alone settles nothing, so we apply a second gate

A domain scoring 15 with a real audience is a real link. A domain scoring 50 with no traffic usually is not. Screening NMI's profile on traffic gives a clear read.

The strong end of that segment includes Wikipedia, Adobe, GitHub, Forbes, Shopify, Salesforce, Bloomberg, TechCrunch, Mastercard, Crunchbase, PYMNTS and Payments Dive. The links NMI has earned are good ones. There are not many of them, and almost all of them point at the homepage.

One number that distorts every headline

NMI carries 22.8 million backlinks from 4,968 domains. That is roughly 4,605 links per domain, against 24 for Adyen. Nofollow sits at 0.17 percent where a naturally earned profile runs between 15 and 30 percent. Image links account for 31.8 percent of the total.

That pattern is the powered by NMI badge sitting on merchant checkouts, counted millions of times over. It inflates the backlink number and contributes very little authority. We would advise against reporting on total backlinks for this account at all.

The network runs between DR 32 and DR 74. It would clear almost any screen built on score alone. Nearly every domain in it has no organic traffic whatsoever.

An authority score filter passes all 327 of these domains. Adding a threshold of 1,000 monthly organic visits removes 315 of them.

This is why we screen on score, traffic and anchor profile together rather than score by itself. NMI's own data makes the argument better than we could.

Flag one. A third party spam anchor is live on the profile

An anchor advertising a black hat link service through Telegram appears across 100 referring domains. First seen in October 2025 and still active. NMI did not place these. At current volume the exposure is small, but nobody is watching it and it is growing.

Flag two. A cluster of misspelled brand variants needs ownership verification

Around a dozen typo variants of NMI's acquired brands, USAePay and Network Merchants, appear with unusually uniform link volumes. These are either NMI's own defensive registrations or a third party network. We can establish which. It needs an ownership check rather than a guess.

We have not measured penalty risk and we are not claiming any. That assessment belongs in a full audit.

05  Opportunity

Every competitor holds developer links. NMI holds none.

NMI sells embedded payments to developers in the United States. Adyen, Authorize.Net, Checkout.com and Paysafe all carry links from package registries, integration directories, technology profilers and developer communities. NMI carries none of them.

We ran this twice. Once as a Semrush backlink matrix across four competitors, and once as an independent Ahrefs referring domain comparison filtered to followed, in content links from domains with real traffic. Both returned the same answer.

The category maps onto the people NMI sells to, costs very little to acquire, and returns qualified referral traffic alongside authority. It is the fastest available win in the profile and nobody is currently taking it.

Where the rest of the pool sits

Beyond the developer set, the qualified pool breaks into payments and fintech peers, specialist trade press, and comparison and review sites. Each group is already demonstrably willing to link to companies in this category, which removes most of the guesswork from prospecting.

They do not need volume. The payment gateway and NMI Payments pages already hold 63 and 57 referring domains, which is above the bar these results set. The same two page types the brief asked for, weighted towards where the return actually sits.

07  Proposal

Four link types, one quality floor.

 Link typeTarget scoreWhy it earns its place
01Fintech and payments trade press30 to 60Closes the AS 30 and above gap while carrying topical relevance, which is what these results reward
02Developer ecosystem and integration listings30 to 55Matches the buyer, competitors already hold them, low cost and qualified referral traffic
03Comparison, review and software directories25 to 50Commercial intent, so it converts as well as it ranks
04Press built on original research50 and aboveProven for this brand, and it gives Battenhall a press cycle to run

Quality floor. Authority Score 30 as the minimum, the majority at 40 and above, and every domain screened for real organic traffic before we approach it. Below 30 a link does not move the metric you report on, so we would not place it.

On the fourth type, NMI's own history answers the format question

The generative AI in payments piece earned 143 referring domains. The consumer survey earned 42. Original research works for this brand, competitors cannot copy the data, and it turns the overlap between our two workstreams into a reason to run them together rather than side by side.

What we would deliberately leave alone

  • Badge, footer and template placements. NMI already carries 22.8 million backlinks from 4,968 domains. More of the same moves nothing.
  • Low authority directory submissions. The bottom band already accounts for 57 percent of the profile.
  • Anything that adds backlink count without adding a new referring domain at Authority Score 30 or above.

A quick win we would hand over regardless

Roughly 1,900 of NMI's referring domains point at five duplicate privacy policy URLs. Consolidating them recovers authority that already exists and makes every link built afterwards work harder. It sits outside this programme, but it should be done and we will show you exactly where.

How we would measure it

What we commit to

  • Editorial referring domains won each month, split by Authority Score band
  • Referring domains gained per target page against the map in section 06
  • Position movement on the named target keywords

What we would advise against

  • Total backlinks. 22.8 million of them are one integration counted over and over
  • Total referring domains. The profile already gains around 113 a month with no help from anyone
  • Total organic traffic. Login pages alone pull more than 4,000 visits a month

Before the first month we would establish a baseline that separates editorially earned domains from the passive badge footprint. Without that split, the programme gets measured against growth it did not create.

What we would need to start

  • NMI's product pages ranked in commercial priority order
  • Agreement on who produces placement content, Battenhall or ourselves
  • Any link history worth knowing about, including prior agencies, disavow files and manual actions
  • Confirmation of target markets beyond the United States

This is a snapshot rather than an audit.
