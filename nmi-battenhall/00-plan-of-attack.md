## 3. Five findings that should drive the pitch

### F1 — These SERPs are gated by *domain* authority, not page-level links
The pages ranking top 10 for the target head terms have almost no links of their own:

- `embedded payments` (US): winning pages carry **1–14 referring domains**. Domain Ratings 51–95.
  NMI already ranks **#2** with a page that has **5 referring domains**.
- `payment processing` (US): winning pages carry **2–80 referring domains** (excluding Wikipedia
  at 427 and Helcim's homepage at 1,611). Domain Ratings 74–99.

**Implication:** a plan that pours 30 links into one product page is wasting budget. The page-level
bar is low — roughly **8–15 quality referring domains is enough to out-link almost any page on
these SERPs**. What NMI actually needs is (a) continued domain-level authority growth and (b) a
*thin, well-distributed* page-level layer. That is a much more efficient use of £4–5K than the
usual "links to product pages" model, and it is a strong, evidence-led differentiator in the pitch.

### F2 — NMI's existing authority is trapped in the wrong URLs
Of ~4,164 referring domains:

- **2,120** point at the homepage (153,459 links — the passive "powered by NMI" gateway/checkout footprint)
- **~1,900** point at **five duplicate privacy-policy URLs**: `/policy/privacy/` (531),
  `/legal/privacy-policy/` (504), `/policy/privacy-policy/` (504), `/privacy-policy` (260),
  `/privacy-policy/` (111)
- **63** point at `/products/payment-gateway/`; **63** at `/products/nmi-payments/`; **32** at `/blog/`

**Implication:** there is a free win here before a single new link is bought — consolidate the
duplicate privacy-policy URLs and audit internal linking from the homepage into money pages.
We should scope this as a small advisory line item. It costs us little, it builds credibility with
Battenhall, and it makes every link we subsequently build work harder.

### F3 — The money is in striking-distance blog posts, not product pages
Eight NMI pages sit at **positions 2–12** on commercially valuable terms, carrying a combined
**~$22.8k/month of traffic value**, held up by an average of **4.6 referring domains each**:

| Page | Position | Traffic value /mo | Ref. domains |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/blog/the-ultimate-white-label-payment-gateway-introduction.../` | 5 | $10,035 | **1** |
| `/blog/payfacs-the-ins-and-outs-of-the-payment-facilitator-model/` | 6 | $4,030 | **3** |
| `/blog/what-are-embedded-payments/` | 2 | $3,625 | **4** |
| `/solutions/unattended-self-service-payments/` | 2 | $1,411 | 12 |
| `/blog/navigating-the-merchant-onboarding-process/` | 5 | $1,327 | **1** |
| `/blog/subscription-payments-101-.../` | 7 | $994 | **3** |
| `/blog/how-contactless-payments-work-and-work-securely/` | 12 | $950 | 8 |
| `/blog/top-5-tips-for-reducing-payments-fraud/` | 10 | $386 | **5** |

The contactless post is the standout volume play: **position 12 for a 20,000/mo keyword**.
Page one is a handful of links away.

**Implication:** this is the spine of the link plan. It answers the client's "blog" requirement,
it is measurable within the retainer period, and it produces revenue-relevant movement far faster
than blanket product-page linking. Product pages still get an allocation — see F1 — but they are
not where the first 90 days should be weighted.

### F4 — Stripe, Adyen and Authorize.Net are brand competitors, not SERP competitors
On the actual target SERPs, the pages beating NMI are GoCardless, Adobe, Helcim, SVB, Alacriti,
WEX, Wikipedia and Reddit. Meanwhile the realistic *authority* benchmark set is
Checkout.com (DR 80), Paysafe (DR 80), Finix (DR 66), Payrix (DR 66).

**Implication:** we should present two competitor frames in the pitch and be explicit about it —
the brand set the client named, and the SERP set that actually determines rankings. Benchmarking
NMI's link programme against Stripe would set a target we cannot hit and should not sell.

### F5 — NMI already has link velocity, so we need a clean attribution baseline
Referring domains grew **2,719 → 4,070 in the last 12 months (~113/month net)**, accelerating
since February 2026.

**Implication:** if we do not baseline this properly, SUSO will be measured against a growth curve
we did not create — or worse, credited for it and then held to it. Much of that growth is likely
the passive merchant/gateway footprint, not editorial links. **Before the pitch we must segment
editorial referring domains from the passive footprint and quote our targets in editorial RDs only.**
This is a research gap — see section 4.

## 6. Scope architecture (£5K vs £4K)

**Now built out in `02-scope-and-target-map.md`.** Decisions taken:

- **D1 — Lead with striking distance.** ~70% of allocation to the positions 2–12 pages in the
  first 90 days, ~30% thin relevance-led layer to product pages.
- **D2 — Say so openly in the pitch.** We present the evidence for re-weighting the brief rather
  than quietly deviating from it.
- **D3 — Volumes come from the SUSO rate card**, not from benchmark assumptions. All volume cells
  in the scope tables are currently blank.
- **D4 — Content production ownership goes to Battenhall as an explicit question.** Both cost
  models (SUSO produces / Battenhall produces) are drafted so we can price either.

**The honest lever between the two scopes is volume and link-magnet cadence, not quality.** In US
fintech/payments, dropping the DR floor to preserve a link count is how this account gets lost at
month four.

**Recommended framing for the £4K version:** the same strategy at a slower cadence with a longer
runway to the same KPIs — *not* a cheaper, lighter product.

## 7. Things to challenge in the brief

- **"Links to product pages and blog"** is the wrong altitude for the ask. Per F1 and F3, the
  efficient plan weights heavily toward striking-distance pages first, with a thin product-page
  layer. We should say this in the pitch rather than quietly doing something else.
- **£5K/month is modest for US fintech link acquisition.** Payments is a premium vertical
  (CPCs of $11–25 on the target terms). We should be explicit about realistic monthly link
  volumes rather than let Battenhall infer a bigger number.
- **NMI's existing velocity (F5) is a trap.** Do not accept a KPI expressed as total referring
  domains.
- **Duplicate-URL and internal linking issues (F2) may not be SUSO's to fix** — but if nobody
  fixes them, our links underperform. Flag ownership explicitly in the proposal.
- **Battenhall is a comms/PR agency.** There is a real risk of overlap between their digital PR
  output and our link acquisition. Resolve R5 before scoping, or we will end up either
  double-charging or fighting over attribution.
