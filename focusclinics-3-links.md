Laser eye surgery, London. Top pages are blog content — "david bowie eyes", "eye colour
meaning", "jaundice eyes", "milia" — classic digital-PR bait, which is why the profile carries
real editorial links from `dailymail.co.uk`, `telegraph.co.uk`, `independent.co.uk`,
`cosmopolitan.com`, `menshealth.com`, `vogue.co.uk`, `which.co.uk`, `bupa.co.uk` and
`nationalgeographic.com`.

Traffic splits **US 2,759 / GB 2,366** — the blog ranks internationally, but the business is
London. Commercial relevance is UK; informational reach is US.

**1,477 live referring domains.** Note 1,008,411 all-time backlinks against 3,303 live, and
5,111 all-time referring domains against 1,477 — a large historic spam footprint has decayed.

geographic relevance. The client is a London clinic, so an all-international basket would leave
the commercial market unserved.

## What I deliberately avoided

**High-DR international news.** `dnaindia.com` $450 (AS 65, TF 62), `deccanchronicle.com` $350
(AS 64), `israelnationalnews.com` $250 (TF 64), `thenationonlineng.net` $250 (AS 63) all beat my
picks on raw metrics. For a **YMYL medical** site they are the wrong buy: zero commercial
relevance to a London eye clinic, and a cluster of paid links from unrelated overseas news
portals is exactly the footprint Google scrutinises hardest in health. Authority you cannot
justify topically is a liability here, not an asset.

**`noodle-magazine.co.uk` $130** (AS 34, 66k traffic, UK lifestyle/health) looked like cheap UK
inventory. The name closely tracks `noodlemagazine.com`, an adult video-streaming domain that
also appears in this sheet. I have not confirmed a relationship, but for a medical client that
similarity alone is reason enough to skip it.

**University domains** (`essex.ac.uk` $250 and similar) — an obvious paid-link footprint.

**`addmagazine.co.uk` $30** — the same guest-post vendor inventory I flagged on two other
accounts in this session.

## One caveat before you buy

UK laser eye surgery advertising sits under both CAP/ASA rules and GMC guidance on advertising
medical services — claims about outcomes, safety and pricing are constrained. That affects what
the placed article can say more than whether the link is accepted, but brief the writer
accordingly. Metrics are the marketplace sheet's own figures, not re-verified per domain, and I
contacted no publisher.
