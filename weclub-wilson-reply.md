**1. High backlink count vs. lower referring domains — does this make sense?**

Yes, and it's expected. "Backlinks" counts every individual link, while
"referring domains" counts the unique websites those links sit on — so a single
domain can generate many backlinks (from different pages, or from an element
that repeats across a whole site, like a footer, resource list or author box).
SEMrush currently shows roughly 542,000 backlinks from about 3,270 referring
domains. A ratio like that is normal when a large share of links are site-wide
or auto-generated — one site links thousands of times. So yes, many of those
backlinks do come from the same domains, and the total link count on its own
isn't the quality signal; the quality and diversity of the *referring domains*
is what matters, and that's what we manage on the links we build. Happy to share
the referring-domain breakdown so you can see the split.

**2. DA/DR of the domains, and do we check authority before using them?**

We vet every domain before a link is placed — but we deliberately don't lean on
DA (Moz) or DR (Ahrefs) as the primary gate. Both can be artificially inflated;
it's relatively easy to pump DR/DA on an otherwise low-quality site, so they can
give a misleading picture of real authority. Instead we screen primarily on
Semrush's **Authority Score (AS)**, which is derived from a broader mix of
signals — link quality and quantity, organic search traffic and natural traffic
patterns — and is much harder to game. We apply a minimum AS threshold and also
check the domain's organic traffic and topical relevance before we use it.

One thing worth noting: the Authority Score you see on the domain as a whole
(currently 26) reflects the *entire* link profile — including all the external
links the site has picked up on its own, which I'll come to below — not just the
ones we build. Our vetting threshold applies to what we place, not to what
arrives externally.

**3. Same IP / same network concern**

You're right on the principle — if referring domains sit on the same IP or in
the same network, Google can treat them as effectively one source and discount
the extra "domains." That's exactly why, on the links we build, we work for
genuine diversity across domains, hosts and networks rather than volume from a
connected footprint. The links we place are not coming from a single network of
sites on shared IPs.

**4. The "suspicious network patterns" you're seeing on the primary domain in
SEMrush**

This is the most important thing to separate out, and I want to be transparent
about it: you're not imagining the clustering — the aggregate profile does show
groups of referring domains sitting on shared networks. But that clustering is
**not coming from us.**

Every established domain accumulates a large volume of links it never asked for:
scraper and mirror sites, auto-generated directories, aggregators, and in some
cases spam or negative-SEO links. These are exactly the kind of links that
repeat site-wide (driving that very high backlink-to-domain ratio) and cluster
onto shared IPs and interlinked hosts — which is what surfaces as a "network
graph" pattern in SEMrush. They are external and unsolicited, and in most cases
they pre-date or run entirely parallel to our work.

So there's an important distinction here between **what we are actively doing** —
a controlled, vetted, network-diverse set of links that we track directly — and
**what is appearing on the domain from external factors outside our control.**
The overall SEMrush picture blends the two together, so it shouldn't be read as
a reflection of what we're building.
